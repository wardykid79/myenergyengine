Heat pump vs oil boiler: running costs in Ireland

A heat pump can be 20–30% cheaper to run than oil. Or no cheaper at all. The deciding factor isn't the technology — it's your house.

Updated June 20266 min readIndependent — we sell nothing

Oil heats more Irish homes than anything else, especially outside the cities. So the real question for hundreds of thousands of households is simple: would a heat pump actually cost less to run? Here's the honest maths.

The two numbers behind everything

Forget the brochures. Running cost comes down to the price of the fuel and how efficiently your system turns it into heat:

  • Oil boiler: oil is around €1.30 a litre in 2026. A good boiler is about 86% efficient, so each useful unit of heat costs roughly 14–15 cent.
  • Heat pump: electricity is around 35 cent a unit, but a heat pump produces about 3 units of heat per unit of electricity in a decent home — so each useful unit of heat costs roughly 10–12 cent.

On those numbers the heat pump wins on running cost — by something like 20–30%. But that "3 units of heat" figure is the catch. It only holds in a home that keeps the heat in.

A worked example

Take a typical three-bed home needing about 15,000 kWh a year for heating and hot water. Here's roughly how the annual running cost compares — and how badly it changes in a leaky house:

⚠ Why the leaky house flips

In a draughty home a heat pump has to work harder and run hotter, so its efficiency drops from ~3 down toward ~2 or below. At that point the running cost can match or beat your oil bill the wrong way. This is exactly why fitting a heat pump before insulating is the most common expensive mistake.

The bits the running-cost table doesn't show

  • Carbon tax keeps rising on oil through to 2030, so the oil column quietly gets worse every year.
  • Price stability: oil swings hard with global markets; electricity is steadier, and steadier still if you add solar.
  • Grants: switching off oil unlocks up to €12,500 in SEAI support toward the heat pump — which the running-cost comparison alone ignores.
  • Comfort: a heat pump gives steady, all-day warmth rather than blasts of heat — different, and most people prefer it once used to it.

The honest verdict

For a reasonably insulated Irish home on oil, a heat pump is usually cheaper to run, cleaner, and increasingly the smart long-term call — especially with carbon tax climbing and a €12,500 grant on the table. For a leaky home, the answer is "not yet" — insulate first, then the heat pump becomes a clear win rather than a gamble.

So the comparison that actually matters isn't heat pump vs oil in general. It's both options, run against your specific house, in the right order.

Common questions

Is a heat pump cheaper to run than oil in Ireland?
In a reasonably insulated home, usually yes — often 20–30% cheaper. In a poorly insulated home the efficiency drops and the running cost can match or exceed oil.
What does it cost to heat an Irish home with oil in 2026?
At about €1.30 a litre, a reasonably insulated three-bed might spend €1,800–€2,400 a year on oil for heat and hot water. A leaky home can cost far more.
Should I just replace my old oil boiler with a new one?
It's the cheapest fix short-term, but it locks you into oil and rising carbon costs for 15+ years, with no grant. Often insulation plus a heat pump pays back better — check your own numbers first.
