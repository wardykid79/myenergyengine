Heat pump grants in Scotland 2026: why they beat England's

Scotland doesn't use the Boiler Upgrade Scheme everyone talks about. It has its own scheme — and for many homes, it's quietly the better deal in Great Britain.

Updated June 20266 min readIndependent — we sell nothing

If you're a Scottish homeowner reading about the "£7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme," here's the first thing to know: that scheme isn't yours. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is England and Wales only. Scotland runs Home Energy Scotland instead — and in several ways it's more generous.

What Scotland actually offers in 2026

The Home Energy Scotland Grant and Loan gives you a mix of free grant money and an optional interest-free loan:

  • Heat-pump grant: up to £7,500 — rising to £9,000 if your home qualifies for the rural and island uplift.
  • An interest-free loan for the rest of the cost — up to a further £7,500 — repayable over several years, with a small (1.5%) admin fee on the loan.
  • Insulation funded up to 75% of the cost, to a maximum grant of £7,500 (£9,000 rural) — with a loan available for the remainder.
  • 0% VAT on heat pumps, insulation and solar, UK-wide.
The big difference

Scotland's heat-pump grant is not means-tested. Any homeowner whose property is their main residence can apply — you don't need to be on a low income. That alone puts it ahead of Northern Ireland, where most owners get nothing.

Three ways it beats the English scheme

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) in England and Wales is a flat £7,500 grant, and that's it. Home Energy Scotland does more:

  1. A rural uplift. Remote, rural and island homes get £9,000, not £7,500. BUS has no such uplift.
  2. An interest-free loan on top. BUS gives you the grant and nothing else. Scotland lets you spread the remaining cost at 0% interest — so the upfront hit is far smaller.
  3. Insulation is funded too. In England, insulation help is mostly the means-tested ECO4 scheme. In Scotland, insulation comes through the same grant-and-loan package, open to ordinary homeowners.

The one catch worth knowing

There are two things to keep in mind. First, the solar PV grant closed in 2024 — solar is now loan-only in Scotland (though 0% VAT still applies). Second, the process is homeowner-led: unlike BUS, where the installer claims the grant for you, in Scotland you contact Home Energy Scotland first and follow the steps in your report. A little more legwork, but the funding is better.

⚠ The mistake to avoid — same everywhere

Generous grant or not, fitting a heat pump into a draughty, poorly insulated home is still the costliest mistake in this whole area. Insulate first, confirm the house can run a heat pump efficiently, then install. Scotland funds the insulation too — so there's no excuse to skip it.

So is it worth it in Scotland?

For a reasonably insulated Scottish home — especially a rural one on oil — the combination of a £9,000 grant, an interest-free loan and funded insulation makes a heat pump one of the strongest moves you can make in 2026. For a leaky home, the answer is the same as anywhere: insulate first (Scotland will help pay), then add the heat pump. The real question is what that looks like for your specific house, and in what order.

Common questions

Does the Boiler Upgrade Scheme work in Scotland?
No. The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme is England and Wales only. Scotland has Home Energy Scotland, which works differently and is often more generous.
How much is the heat pump grant in Scotland in 2026?
Up to £7,500, rising to £9,000 with the rural and island uplift, plus an optional interest-free loan for the rest of the cost. It isn't means-tested.
Can I get a solar grant in Scotland?
The solar PV grant closed in 2024 — solar is now funded by interest-free loan only. 0% VAT still applies, saving roughly £1,200–£2,000 on a typical install.
Who can apply?
Homeowners in Scotland where the property is their only or main residence. Landlords, businesses and developers are excluded.
