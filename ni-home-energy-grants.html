Home energy grants in Northern Ireland: the honest guide

No Boiler Upgrade Scheme. No SEAI. So what can a Northern Ireland homeowner actually get in 2026 — and where's the gap nobody talks about?

Updated June 20266 min readIndependent — we sell nothing

If you live in Northern Ireland and you've been reading about generous heat-pump grants, here's the hard truth most websites won't tell you plainly: most of those schemes don't apply to you.

The big numbers you see — £7,500 in England, up to €12,500 in the Republic — stop at the border. A home a few miles north of Lifford or Strabane can be almost identical to one a few miles south, and qualify for thousands less. That isn't a glitch. It's just how the schemes are drawn up. This guide lays out what you can really claim in 2026, in plain English, with no sales pitch attached.

What Northern Ireland does not have

Two of the most generous schemes on these islands simply don't reach you:

  • The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) — the £7,500 grant for air- and ground-source heat pumps — covers England and Wales only. It does not operate in Northern Ireland.
  • SEAI grants — the Republic's package worth up to €12,500 for a heat pump, plus grants for insulation and solar — apply only in the Republic of Ireland. Living in NI, you cannot claim them.
⚠ The honest gap

This is the single most important thing to understand before you spend money: a standard owner-occupier in Northern Ireland, above the low-income thresholds, gets no direct grant toward a heat pump. Don't budget as if you'll get £7,500 — you won't.

What you can actually get in 2026

Support in NI is almost entirely means-tested — aimed at lower-income and vulnerable households rather than the general homeowner. The main routes:

NISEP — the NI Sustainable Energy Programme

NISEP is funded through a levy on energy bills and delivered by the energy suppliers. It funds insulation and heating measures, with a large share ring-fenced for lower-income households. If your household income is below the qualifying threshold (roughly under £28,000 for a single-person household, with wider criteria for families — always check the current rules), you may get measures fully or heavily funded. Above that, the help thins out fast.

The Affordable Warmth Scheme

Run through local councils and the Housing Executive, Affordable Warmth targets low-income owner-occupiers and some private renters with insulation, heating and efficiency upgrades. Again, it's income-assessed — typically aimed at households under a set income ceiling.

0% VAT — the one thing everyone gets

Here's the universal win: the UK-wide zero rate of VAT on energy-saving materials applies in Northern Ireland too. Heat pumps, solar panels, insulation and battery storage are charged at 0% VAT (rather than 20%) when installed. On a £14,000 heat pump that's a real saving of a couple of thousand pounds — not a grant, but genuine money off.

The bottom line for most owners

If you're a working household above the means-tested thresholds, your realistic 2026 support is 0% VAT and not much else. Plan your budget around the full cost, and treat any means-tested help you do qualify for as a bonus.

So is a heat pump still worth it in NI?

Often, yes — but for different reasons than in the South. Northern Ireland is the most oil-dependent region in these islands: a large majority of homes heat with oil, and oil prices swing hard. A heat pump can still cut your running costs and shield you from those swings — if your home is well enough insulated to run one efficiently. In a draughty, poorly insulated house, a heat pump can disappoint badly, grant or no grant.

That's the real question to answer before you spend: not "what grant can I get?" but "will a heat pump actually run well in my house, and in what order should I do the work?" Insulating first is usually the cheapest, lowest-risk money you can spend — and it's exactly the step most people skip.

Worth knowing

Because NI has no heat-pump grant for most owners, the sequence matters even more here. Spend your own money in the wrong order and there's no grant to soften the mistake. Insulate, confirm the house can run a heat pump, then install — in that order.

Common questions

Is there a Boiler Upgrade Scheme in Northern Ireland?
No. The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme covers England and Wales only. Northern Ireland has no direct equivalent for owner-occupiers who aren't on a low income.
Can I get an SEAI grant in Northern Ireland?
No. SEAI grants apply only in the Republic of Ireland — even if your home is just over the border and nearly identical to one that qualifies in the South.
What can I actually get in NI in 2026?
Mainly means-tested help through NISEP and the Affordable Warmth Scheme for lower-income households, plus the UK-wide 0% VAT on energy-saving materials, which everyone gets.
Is a heat pump worth it without a grant?
It can be — especially given NI's heavy reliance on oil — but only if your home is insulated well enough to run one efficiently. In a leaky house it may not save money. Check your specific home before committing.
